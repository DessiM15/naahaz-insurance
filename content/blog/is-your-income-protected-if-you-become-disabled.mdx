Most people insure their house and their car without thinking twice, then leave
the thing that pays for both completely uninsured. Your ability to earn is
almost certainly your largest financial asset — and it is far more likely to be
interrupted than your house is to burn down.

## The risk is not what people picture

When people imagine disability they picture a catastrophic accident. In practice
the overwhelming majority of long-term disability claims come from ordinary
illness: musculoskeletal problems, cancer, heart conditions, mental health.
Things that arrive quietly and stay for months or years.

## What happens to the money

Run the arithmetic honestly. If your income stopped next month:

- How long could you cover the mortgage and everyday costs from savings?
- What happens to retirement contributions during that period?
- Does your spouse's income cover the gap, and for how long?
- Do medical costs rise at the same time income falls?

For most households the honest answer is measured in months, not years — and a
long-term disability claim frequently outlasts it.

## Employer coverage is usually thinner than assumed

Group long-term disability through work is genuinely valuable, but it typically
has limits worth knowing about:

**It replaces a percentage, not all of it.** Usually around 60% of base salary,
and often excluding bonus or commission — which for many people is a
considerable share of actual earnings.

**It's frequently taxable.** If your employer pays the premium, benefits are
generally taxable to you. That 60% can land closer to 45% once tax is applied.

**It stops when the job does.** Group coverage is tied to employment. Change
jobs and it doesn't come with you.

**There are caps.** Many group plans cap the monthly benefit, which bites
hardest on higher earners.

## What individual coverage adds

An individual policy sits on top of group coverage and closes the gap. Because
you pay the premium with after-tax money, benefits are generally received
tax-free. It's portable, so it follows you between jobs. And the definition of
disability can be stronger — "own occupation" coverage pays if you can't perform
your specific job, rather than requiring that you be unable to do any work at
all. That distinction matters enormously for specialists.

## The uncomfortable part

Disability insurance is priced on health and age, and both move in one
direction. The coverage is cheapest and easiest to qualify for at exactly the
point in life when it feels least necessary.
